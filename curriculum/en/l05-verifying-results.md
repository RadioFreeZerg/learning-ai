# Lesson 5 · Checking results: mistakes, inventions and sources

## Objectives

By the end you will be able to audit an AI answer: what to check, how, with what level of rigor and how to put it in writing.

## Why it matters

The assistant is not accountable for the result; you are. Verification is the skill that separates using AI with judgment from using it blindly, and it is the backbone of the whole course.

## Key concepts

**Types of error.** Invented fact, nonexistent quote or source, wrong calculation, outdated information, important omission, overgeneralization and bias.

**Verifying is your job.** The level of rigor depends on the risk of using the answer. Not everything needs the same care.

**Risk traffic light.** Green: it serves as a draft and a mistake costs little. Yellow: other people will read it; verify the key points. Red: it affects decisions, money, health, rights or reputation; verify everything with primary sources and consult an expert if needed.

**Checking methods.** Compare with the original source (official document, book, regulation, institution's website); search for the fact in a regular search engine with different words; redo the calculations by hand or with a calculator; check that a cited source exists and says what is claimed; ask an expert.

**Asking the assistant for certainty is not enough.** Its saying "I am sure" is not evidence. You can ask it to separate what it states from what it assumes: that guides you but does not replace checking.

**Document what you did.** Note what you checked, how, the result and what was left unverified. That way another person can trust your work or repeat it.

## Guided activity

1. Ask the assistant for an explanation with figures or sources on a topic in your field.
2. Underline five checkable claims and classify the final use of the answer with the traffic light.
3. Check each claim with a method different from the chat: search engine, official document or calculation.
4. Look up whether each cited source really exists and says what is attributed to it.
5. Record the result: what was right, what was wrong and what you could not check.

## Common mistakes

- **Verifying only what looks odd.** The most dangerous errors sound normal.
- **Using the same assistant to verify itself.** It can repeat the error in other words.
- **Checking that the source exists and not that it says what is claimed.**
- **Not noting what was left unchecked.** What is unverified is also information.
