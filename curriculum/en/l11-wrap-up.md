# L11 · Wrap-up: revisiting warnings and a continuity plan

## Objectives

By the end you will have closed the pending items of the course that you decide to revisit, you will know what you have learned and you will have a realistic plan for continuing to use AI with judgment.

## Why it matters

The course ends, but tools change. What lasts is the method: ask well, verify and decide responsibly. A simple plan keeps it from fading.

## Key concepts

**Reviewing warnings.** The warnings saved in your progress mark exercises that you advanced without passing. Revisiting them is an option, not an obligation; those you do not revisit remain recorded.

**Learning review.** Compare your starting point (your initial profile) with what you know and do now: what changed in how you use AI, what is still hard for you and what is missing.

**Habits worth keeping.** Clear context in each instruction, verification according to risk, data protection before pasting and human oversight of what matters.

**Keeping up to date.** Tools and their terms change. Choose two or three reliable sources and set aside time periodically to review news and privacy terms.

**Continuity plan.** Three concrete goals for the next three months, each with an action, a date and a way to check progress.

**Share what you learned.** Explaining to another person what you have learned and showing them your workflow consolidates learning and spreads responsible use.

## Guided activity

1. Review your progress: lessons, exercises and pending warnings.
2. Choose which warnings you will revisit now, which you will leave for later and which you drop with a reason.
3. Compare your initial profile with your current situation and write three concrete changes.
4. Write three goals for the next three months, with action, date and way to check them.
5. Choose a person with whom you will share what you learned and what you will show them.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving warnings unreviewed.** Even if you decide not to revisit them, it is worth deciding consciously.
- **A plan that is too ambitious.** Three small goals are worth more than ten big ones.
- **Goals without a date or a check.** They cannot be followed.
- **Considering the course finished and ceasing to verify.** The habit is what matters.
