# Lesson 1 · What AI is and is not

## Objectives

By the end you will be able to describe clearly what an AI assistant is, what it is useful for in your field and where to be careful, and you will have your own observations from a real conversation to use in exercise E1.

## Why it matters

Someone who expects AI to "know" or "think" like a person trusts it too much and does not verify; someone who dismisses it entirely misses useful help. With a correct idea of what it is and is not, you decide better when to rely on it and when not to.

## Key concepts

**Traditional program versus AI.** A traditional program follows rules written by a person: if A happens, do B. An AI system learns patterns from many examples and applies them to new cases. That is why it can be wrong in ways a rule-based program cannot.

**AI, automation and assistant.** Automating means repeating fixed steps without intervention. An AI assistant converses and produces text, summaries, ideas or analysis from what you ask. An automation always does the same thing; an assistant may answer differently each time.

**Generative AI.** It creates new content (text, images, audio) by imitating the patterns it saw while learning. It produces something plausible; it does not look things up in a list of truths.

**What it usually does well.** Drafting and rephrasing, summarizing, classifying, proposing ideas and structures, translating, explaining something in several ways and helping you organize a problem.

**What it usually does badly.** Guaranteeing that what it says is true, knowing what happened yesterday unless you tell it, knowing your context unless you share it, taking responsibility for a result and deciding for you when values are at stake.

**It predicts, it does not understand like a person.** An assistant produces the answer that is most likely given what it learned. It can sound confident and be wrong. In Lesson 2 you will see why.

**Common myths.** "It knows what is true", "it thinks like a person", "it always gives the same answer", "if it is paid it does not make mistakes", "it replaces human judgment". All are false or incomplete.

## Guided activity

1. Tell your assistant in three sentences what you do and ask for three tasks in your field where AI helps well and two where you should be careful.
2. Pick one of the careful tasks, ask it to do it and observe where it oversimplifies, invents or gets things wrong.
3. Ask it a question about a very recent or very specific fact in your field and observe whether it admits what it does not know.
4. Ask the same question twice, the second time in a separate chat, and compare the answers.
5. Write down three observations: one thing it did well, one it did badly and one that surprised you. You will use them in exercise E1.

## Common mistakes

- **Thinking "AI" is a single thing.** There are very different systems; this course focuses on assistants that converse.
- **Taking a confident tone as a sign of truth.** A well-written text can contain errors.
- **Asking the assistant to judge its own reliability and believing it without more.** Its answer about itself is also a prediction.
- **Assuming the assistant knows your organization.** It only knows what you tell it in the conversation.
