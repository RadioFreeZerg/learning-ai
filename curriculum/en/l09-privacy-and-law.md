# Lesson 9 · Privacy, intellectual property and the legal landscape

Act as the tutor: first read https://github.com/RadioFreeZerg/learning-ai/blob/main/AGENTS.md and follow it, then use the block below as your instructions and teach in short messages. The sections addressed to the student are background only.

## Objectives

By the end you will be able to decide what data you can share with an assistant, what care generated content needs and where to find out about the rules that apply to you.

## Why it matters

A piece of data shared by mistake cannot be taken back. And using content without knowing whose it is or what rules govern it can cause problems for you or your organization.

## Key concepts

**What happens to what you type.** What you send to an assistant leaves your device and is processed on third-party systems. Depending on the service and its settings, it may be stored or used to improve it. Review the terms and privacy settings of the tool you use.

**Data traffic light.** Green: public or invented information, which you can share. Yellow: internal, low-sensitivity information; share it only anonymized or with permission. Red: identifiable personal data, health or financial data, trade secrets or confidential data of third parties; do not share it.

**Minimize and anonymize.** Share the minimum, replace names and identifying data with placeholders and remove what is not needed.

**Intellectual property.** Other people's texts, images and works may be protected. Do not paste protected third-party material for it to reproduce, and check that generated content does not copy existing works.

**Authorship and use of generated content.** The rules about who is the author of AI-made content depend on the country and change. Check the regulations and the tool's terms before commercial or public use.

**Data protection.** Many countries regulate the processing of personal data. Sharing it with an assistant can count as processing or transfer and may require a legal basis or consent.

**Your organization's rules.** Your company or institution may have AI use policies. They apply and override your preferences.

**When to consult.** For third-party data, decisions with legal effects or doubts about regulations, consult the person in charge of data protection or a legal professional. This course is not legal advice.

## Guided activity

1. Make a list of ten types of information you handle in your work.
2. Classify each one with the traffic light and justify the yellows and reds.
3. Pick a yellow text and anonymize it with placeholders; check that nothing identifiable remains.
4. Look up the privacy policy of your tool and note what it says about storage and use of data.
5. Note three legal doubts in your field and whom you would consult about them.

## Common mistakes

- **Believing that anonymizing is removing only the name.** Other data can identify someone.
- **Assuming generated content is free of rights.** It can resemble protected works.
- **Ignoring your organization's policy.** It can be grounds for sanction.
- **Treating this course as legal advice.** Consult a professional.
