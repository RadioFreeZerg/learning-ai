# General rubric

It is used to review the ten exercises (E1–E10). Each exercise specifies, in its "Criteria by dimension" section, what a 2 and a 3 look like, without changing the levels or the pass rule.

## Pass rule

An exercise passes if it meets **all the required items** and scores **2 or more in each of the five dimensions**. There is no average score: a 3 in one dimension does not make up for a 1 in another.

## Levels

| Dimension | 0 · Absent | 1 · Beginning | 2 · Sufficient | 3 · Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptual understanding | Does not use the lesson's concepts or uses them wrongly | Repeats definitions without applying them; there are important errors | Explains the concepts in their own words and applies them without errors of substance | Connects concepts, anticipates limits and proposes their own examples |
| Clarity and structure | It is not clear what is being submitted | Loose ideas; hard to follow the thread | Orderly, complete deliverable, readable by a third party | Concise, well organized and ready to use without explanation |
| Verification and rigor | Accepts the AI output without checking anything | Says it was verified, but does not show how or with what | Checks the key points, states sources or method and flags what was not verified | Systematic verification: cross-checks, documents errors found and corrects them |
| Ethics and risks | Mentions no risks or commits one (sensitive data, obvious bias) | Generic mention, unrelated to the case | Identifies the relevant risks of the case and one measure for each | Prioritizes risks, justifies the measures and sets limits of use |
| Applicability | Unrelated to a real task | Generic case, hard to transfer to their field | Applicable to a real task in their field with minor adjustments | Could use it tomorrow; says how to measure whether it works |

## How the tutor scores

1. First checks the required items: each one is yes or no.
2. Then scores the five dimensions, each with a quote or reference to the submission that justifies it.
3. If in doubt between two levels, asks the student before scoring.
4. Does not pass out of politeness or for effort: scores what was submitted.
5. If not passing, gives 2 to 3 concrete actions, without rewriting the student's work.
6. After the second failed attempt, offers to review again or advance with a warning (see [`docs/review-protocol.en.md`](https://github.com/RadioFreeZerg/learning-ai/blob/main/docs/review-protocol.en.md)).

## A second opinion

An assistant's scoring can vary. The student may paste the same submission and the same review prompt into another assistant and compare; if there are differences, the score the student considers better justified stands.
